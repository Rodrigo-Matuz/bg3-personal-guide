# [[Wizard]]

[[Wizard]] is the most versatile full caster in [[Baldur's Gate 3]], built around an enormous spellbook and the ability to prepare different spells for different situations. Intelligence drives its spellcasting, while its subclass determines what the Wizard does especially well. Depending on the specialization, a Wizard can focus on raw damage, battlefield control, defense, summoning, utility, melee spellblade combat, or even divine and natural magic.

Wizards are particularly strong at adapting to encounters because they can learn spells from scrolls and change their prepared spells outside combat. Their main weakness is that they are generally fragile without defensive tools, and their effectiveness depends heavily on choosing the right spells and using limited spell slots efficiently. The subclasses below come from the base game and modded/expanded BG3 content, so their exact mechanics can vary depending on your modlist. The core Wizard role remains that of an Intelligence-based, highly flexible full caster.

In combat, [[Wizard]] can fill several different roles depending on its subclass:

- **Arcane damage dealer** — Uses powerful spells such as Fireball and other offensive magic to eliminate groups of enemies.
    
- **Battlefield controller** — Uses spells that disable, slow, reposition, or otherwise restrict enemies.
    
- **Defensive caster** — Uses wards, reactions, and defensive magic to survive attacks and protect allies.
    
- **Support caster** — Provides buffs, utility, protection, and specialized magical effects for the party.
    
- **Summoner** — Creates magical creatures or undead servants that add additional bodies and actions to the battlefield.
    
- **Melee spellblade** — Combines Wizard spellcasting with weapons, mobility, and defensive magic.
    
- **Utility specialist** — Uses the Wizard's huge spell selection to solve exploration, environmental, and non-combat problems.
    
- **Debuffer** — Weakens enemies with curses, enchantments, psychic effects, or other magical conditions.
    
- **Information specialist** — Uses divination and magical knowledge to manipulate rolls, reveal information, and prepare for encounters.

## [[Abjuration]]

[[Abjuration]] is the defensive [[Wizard]] specialization. It focuses on wards, magical protection, and preventing hostile effects from reaching you or your allies. Its defining mechanic, Arcane Ward, gives the Wizard an additional layer of protection that can absorb incoming damage, making an otherwise fragile caster substantially harder to kill.

The subclass is particularly useful when your party needs a Wizard who can stay on the battlefield rather than constantly retreating from danger. It still retains the full Wizard spellbook, so defensive specialization does not prevent you from dealing damage or controlling enemies.

**Combat**

[[Abjuration]] spends actions and spell slots normally while using its defensive mechanics to reduce the consequences of being attacked. Abjuration spells help build or maintain its defensive resources, while reactions such as Counterspell let it interfere with enemy spellcasters.

The subclass becomes especially effective when enemies are capable of repeatedly targeting your party. Instead of competing directly with a damage-focused Wizard, it increases the amount of punishment your party can survive.

**Progression**

**Early:**  
Focus on building your defensive tools while maintaining normal Wizard damage and control spells. The subclass is already more forgiving than other Wizard options because its defensive mechanics reduce incoming damage.

**Mid:**  
Your defensive resources become more valuable as encounters become harder and enemies deal more damage. You can afford to remain closer to danger and protect yourself while concentrating on important spells.

**End:**  
The subclass becomes an extremely durable full caster when its defensive mechanics are properly supported. It can maintain strong spellcasting while absorbing punishment that would normally threaten a Wizard.

**Best for:**

- - Defensive Wizard builds
        
- - Protecting fragile party members
        
- - Counterspell-heavy playstyles
        
- - Concentration-focused casters
        
- - Frontline or semi-frontline Wizards
        
- - Players who want maximum survivability

## [[Bladesinging]]

[[Bladesinging]] turns [[Wizard]] into a spellblade that can fight with weapons while retaining full arcane spellcasting. It uses Bladesong to improve mobility, defenses, and concentration, allowing the Wizard to operate much closer to enemies than a traditional caster.

Unlike a conventional Fighter, the subclass still revolves around Wizard spells. Its weapons are another part of its action economy rather than a replacement for spellcasting. In BG3, Bladesinging was officially added with Patch 8 and is restricted from using Bladesong while wearing medium or heavy armor.
**Combat**

[[Bladesinging]] can switch between casting and melee depending on the encounter. Bladesong improves survivability and mobility, allowing the Wizard to enter melee without immediately becoming an easy target.

The major advantage is flexibility: you can use a weapon when conserving spell slots or when melee is advantageous, then switch back to powerful Wizard spells when the situation demands it. The drawback is that the subclass asks you to balance two different combat styles rather than specializing completely in either.

**Progression**

**Early:**  
Bladesong immediately makes the Wizard more capable of fighting near the frontline. Weapon attacks provide a useful alternative when you do not want to spend a spell slot.

**Mid:**  
The combination of stronger Wizard spells and improved Bladesong makes the subclass a genuine hybrid rather than a Wizard merely carrying a sword.

**End:**  
The subclass becomes a highly flexible spellblade capable of combining strong defenses, melee attacks, mobility, and high-level Wizard magic. Its value comes from having multiple ways to spend each turn.

**Best for:**

- - Spellblade builds
        
- - Melee-capable Wizards
        
- - High-mobility casters
        
- - Players who want weapons and spells
        
- - Concentration-heavy melee builds
        
- - Flexible action economy

## [[Chronurgy]]

[[Chronurgy]] specializes in manipulating time and probability. Rather than simply overpowering enemies with damage, this Wizard changes when events happen and whether important actions succeed. It is particularly suited to players who enjoy controlling the outcome of important rolls and disrupting enemy turns.

The subclass works best as a tactical controller. Its power comes from using limited resources at the right moment rather than constantly producing the highest raw damage.

**Combat**

[[Chronurgy]] can interfere with attacks, saving throws, positioning, and other important moments. It is particularly effective when a single failed enemy save or successful ally roll can decide an encounter.

Its action economy is generally focused on normal Wizard spellcasting, with temporal abilities acting as reactions or supplemental effects depending on the implementation. This lets the subclass influence events without necessarily sacrificing its entire turn.

**Progression**

**Early:**  
Use normal Wizard control spells while learning when temporal abilities are worth spending. Avoid wasting limited manipulation effects on insignificant rolls.

**Mid:**  
The subclass becomes increasingly useful in encounters where several important rolls determine the outcome. It can protect allies from bad outcomes while forcing enemies into unfavorable ones.

**End:**  
High-level Chronurgy becomes a powerful control-oriented Wizard capable of manipulating crucial moments rather than simply reacting to them.

**Best for:**

- - Roll manipulation
        
- - Battlefield control
        
- - Tactical players
        
- - Protecting important saving throws
        
- - Disrupting enemy turns
        
- - Fate/time manipulation builds

## [[Conjuration]]

[[Conjuration]] specializes in creating things, summoning creatures, teleporting, and manipulating battlefield positioning. It is one of the most utility-focused Wizard subclasses and can contribute through both direct combat and the environment around the fight.

Its biggest advantage is flexibility. A Conjurer does not necessarily need to win encounters through direct damage because summoned creatures, teleportation, terrain manipulation, and utility effects can all create advantages.

**Combat**

[[Conjuration]] can spend actions creating additional combatants, repositioning allies, or establishing effects that make the battlefield easier to control. Summons can absorb attacks, occupy enemies, block routes, or add additional damage.

The main drawback is action economy: spending an action on setup can delay immediate damage. The subclass becomes more valuable in longer encounters where summoned creatures have time to contribute.

**Progression**

**Early:**  
Use conjuration spells for utility and positioning while supplementing them with conventional Wizard damage and control.

**Mid:**  
Summons and teleportation become more meaningful as encounters become larger. Additional creatures can absorb attacks and create more tactical options.

**End:**  
The subclass becomes highly effective in encounters where multiple summons can exploit the enemy's limited actions. Its strength is creating advantages that persist across several turns.

**Best for:**

- - Summoner Wizards
        
- - Battlefield positioning
        
- - Teleportation
        
- - Utility-heavy builds
        
- - Additional bodies on the battlefield
        
- - Long encounters

## [[Divination]]

[[Divination]] is the information and fate-manipulation specialization of [[Wizard]]. It focuses on predicting events and changing important dice rolls, making it exceptionally useful for ensuring that critical attacks, saving throws, and control effects succeed.

Its strength is not necessarily higher damage. Instead, it improves the reliability of the entire party by allowing you to intervene when an important roll would otherwise fail.

**Combat**

[[Divination]] is primarily a controller and support caster. It can use normal Wizard spells and reserve its divination mechanics for important moments, such as forcing a powerful enemy to fail a saving throw or preventing an ally from failing a crucial check.

Portent-style mechanics are particularly powerful because they can affect the outcome of important rolls rather than simply adding another source of damage. This makes the subclass valuable even when its own turn is spent casting a normal Wizard spell.

**Progression**

**Early:**  
Begin using your prediction abilities on important rolls rather than minor attacks. Your normal Wizard spellbook remains the main source of combat power.

**Mid:**  
The subclass becomes increasingly reliable as encounters become more dependent on powerful saving throws and attack rolls.

**End:**  
[[Divination]] becomes one of the strongest tactical support options for a Wizard because it can directly influence decisive rolls while retaining the complete Wizard spell list.

**Best for:**

- - Spell reliability
        
- - Crowd control
        
- - Support casting
        
- - Manipulating saving throws
        
- - Preventing critical failures
        
- - Tactical players

## [[Enchantment]]

[[Enchantment]] focuses on controlling enemies through charm, incapacitation, and mental manipulation. Rather than killing enemies immediately, the subclass attempts to make them ineffective or turn them against their allies.

It is particularly effective for players who prefer disabling important targets instead of simply dealing the largest possible damage number.

**Combat**

[[Enchantment]] uses actions primarily for control spells. A successful enchantment can effectively remove an enemy from the fight or force them to behave in a way that benefits the party.

The major limitation is saving throws and enemy immunities. A failed control spell can waste your action and spell slot, so choosing targets carefully is important.

**Progression**

**Early:**  
Use basic enchantments to disable dangerous enemies while relying on conventional Wizard spells against targets that are difficult to control.

**Mid:**  
More powerful control effects allow the subclass to influence several enemies and manipulate encounters more consistently.

**End:**  
The subclass becomes a powerful controller capable of neutralizing important targets rather than competing directly with damage-focused casters.

**Best for:**

- - Crowd control
        
- - Mental manipulation
        
- - Charming enemies
        
- - Incapacitating priority targets
        
- - Non-lethal control
        
- - Players who prefer control over damage

## [[Evocation]]

[[Evocation]] is the dedicated offensive [[Wizard]]. It specializes in direct magical damage, particularly large AoE spells, while reducing the risk of damaging allies with your own attacks.

This is the simplest Wizard specialization if your main objective is to cast powerful offensive spells every round. Sculpt Spells is particularly important because it lets allies automatically succeed on saving throws against your evocation spells, making aggressive AoE casting much easier in a crowded battlefield.

**Combat**

[[Evocation]] primarily spends its actions casting offensive spells. Fireballs, lightning effects, cold spells, Magic Missile, and other evocation options become the core of its combat rotation.

Its biggest advantage is that you can cast AoE spells into groups containing your own party without needing to avoid allies as carefully. This gives the Evoker more freedom to use large damage areas aggressively.

**Progression**

**Early:**  
Use cantrips and basic offensive spells while learning to position AoE attacks effectively.

**Mid:**  
Large AoE spells become the core of the subclass. Sculpt Spells makes aggressive spell placement considerably safer.

**End:**  
The subclass becomes a dedicated magical artillery piece capable of producing extremely reliable AoE damage while minimizing friendly-fire concerns.

**Best for:**

- - Pure spell damage
        
- - AoE damage
        
- - Fireball-focused builds
        
- - Magic Missile builds
        
- - Aggressive casters
        
- - Parties with multiple melee characters

## [[Hedge Mage]]

[[Hedge Mage]] combines traditional Wizard study with practical nature magic. Instead of choosing between arcane and druidic utility, this subclass uses both, giving the Wizard access to natural spells, plant-based effects, summons, and alchemical tools.

It is particularly useful outside combat because nature magic can provide exploration utility that a conventional Wizard may not have.

**Combat**

[[Hedge Mage]] can use conventional Wizard spells alongside Druid-style effects. It can manipulate terrain, summon natural creatures, provide support, and deal elemental damage.

Its main advantage is breadth rather than specialization. You gain more ways to solve problems, but you do not necessarily outperform a dedicated Evoker in damage or a dedicated Abjurer in defense.

**Progression**

**Early:**  
Use basic arcane and nature spells to cover utility gaps in the party.

**Mid:**  
The combination of plant effects, summons, and Wizard control becomes more useful as encounters become more complex.

**End:**  
The subclass becomes a highly versatile nature/arcane hybrid capable of filling several party gaps.

**Best for:**

- - Nature-themed Wizards
        
- - Utility-heavy parties
        
- - Plant magic
        
- - Summoning
        
- - Exploration utility
        
- - Arcane/Druid hybrids

## [[Hexcraft]]

[[Hexcraft]] specializes in curses and debilitating magical effects. Rather than maximizing immediate spell damage, it weakens enemies and creates conditions that make them easier for the entire party to kill.

The subclass works particularly well when paired with characters who can repeatedly exploit weakened or cursed targets.

**Combat**

[[Hexcraft]] spends its actions applying curses and debuffs, then uses normal Wizard damage and control spells to exploit those conditions. The strongest turns often involve setting up a target before committing to major damage.

Its drawback is setup time. A pure damage Wizard may kill an enemy immediately, while Hexcraft may need several effects before its specialization reaches maximum value.

**Progression**

**Early:**  
Use simple curses and conventional Wizard attacks while learning which enemies are worth debuffing.

**Mid:**  
Stacking debilitating effects becomes more practical, especially against durable targets that survive multiple rounds.

**End:**  
The subclass becomes a strong debuff specialist capable of turning important enemies into much easier targets for the entire party.

**Best for:**

- - Curse-focused builds
        
- - Debuffing
        
- - Necrotic magic
        
- - Boss fights
        
- - Party damage amplification
        
- - Occult Wizard builds

## [[Hierophant]]

[[Hierophant]] is a Wizard who has studied divine magic through arcane scholarship. It combines Wizard versatility with access to healing, restoration, radiant and necrotic magic, and other traditionally divine effects.

This gives the party access to a caster who can cover magical problems normally handled by a [[Cleric]] without abandoning the Wizard's broad spellbook.

**Combat**

[[Hierophant]] can switch between conventional Wizard casting and divine support depending on the encounter. It can deal damage, disable enemies, heal allies, remove harmful effects, and provide defensive support.

The main drawback is that its broad toolkit can compete for the same actions and spell slots. You should decide whether a given turn is more valuable as damage, control, or support.

**Progression**

**Early:**  
Use Wizard spells as your main combat tools while adding divine utility where the party needs it.

**Mid:**  
Healing, restoration, and divine offensive spells allow the subclass to cover more party roles.

**End:**  
The subclass becomes a highly flexible full caster capable of handling both arcane and divine problems without needing to specialize exclusively in either.

**Best for:**

- - Arcane/divine hybrids
        
- - Party support
        
- - Healing
        
- - Utility casting
        
- - Radiant damage
        
- - Flexible full casters

## [[Illusion]]

[[Illusion]] specializes in deception, invisibility, misdirection, and manipulating enemy perception. Rather than relying exclusively on damage or direct control, it creates situations where enemies struggle to determine what they should attack or where they should move.

It is particularly useful for players who enjoy solving encounters through positioning and deception rather than brute force.

**Combat**

[[Illusion]] uses actions to create deceptive effects, manipulate enemy attention, and establish advantageous positions. Invisibility and illusion effects can help the party initiate fights on favorable terms or disengage from dangerous situations.

Its main weakness is that illusion effects are less direct than damage. Their value depends heavily on encounter layout and how effectively you exploit the confusion they create.

**Progression**

**Early:**  
Focus on simple deception, stealth, and positioning while retaining standard Wizard damage and control spells.

**Mid:**  
Illusions become more useful for manipulating groups and creating openings for other party members.

**End:**  
The subclass becomes a strong utility and deception specialist, particularly in encounters where positioning and enemy perception matter.

**Best for:**

- - Illusion magic
        
- - Stealth support
        
- - Invisibility
        
- - Misdirection
        
- - Creative encounter solutions
        
- - Utility-focused Wizards

## [[Necrobroker]]

[[Necrobroker]] combines necromancy with divination, treating the dead as sources of information and magical resources. It focuses on souls, curses, undead servants, auguries, and discovering weaknesses that can be exploited.

Unlike a pure [[Necromancy]] Wizard, the subclass places greater emphasis on information and manipulation alongside undead magic.

**Combat**

[[Necrobroker]] can use undead summons to increase the party's available actions while supporting them with curses and magical information. The subclass is strongest when it has enough time to establish its undead resources.

The additional bodies can draw enemy attacks, surround targets, block movement, and add damage. The tradeoff is that managing many summons makes combat more complicated.

**Progression**

**Early:**  
Use standard Wizard spells while establishing your first necromantic tools and learning when summons are worth the action economy investment.

**Mid:**  
Undead servants become a meaningful part of combat, while divination and curses provide additional ways to exploit important targets.

**End:**  
The subclass becomes a dedicated necromantic controller capable of combining undead armies with information-gathering and curse mechanics.

**Best for:**

- - Necromancer builds
        
- - Undead armies
        
- - Divination and necromancy hybrids
        
- - Summon-heavy parties
        
- - Curse-focused combat
        
- - Players who enjoy managing multiple units

## [[Necromancy]]

[[Necromancy]] turns death into a combat resource. It specializes in necrotic damage, life-force manipulation, and raising fallen creatures as undead servants.

The subclass is especially effective in parties that can take advantage of additional bodies on the battlefield. Undead can contribute damage, absorb attacks, occupy space, and create action-economy advantages.

**Combat**

[[Necromancy]] normally combines conventional Wizard spells with undead summons. Instead of spending every action directly attacking, you can create additional combatants that continue contributing after the summoning turn.

The downside is management. Large numbers of summons can make turns slower and battlefield positioning more complicated. The subclass also depends heavily on having useful bodies to raise or sufficient spell resources to maintain its army.

**Progression**

**Early:**  
Use necrotic spells and basic undead abilities while still relying heavily on standard Wizard magic.

**Mid:**  
Undead servants become a major part of your combat strategy, giving the party more bodies and more opportunities to control enemy positioning.

**End:**  
The subclass can become an army-building specialist, combining high-level Wizard spells with multiple undead servants.

**Best for:**

- - Undead armies
        
- - Summoner builds
        
- - Necrotic damage
        
- - Action-economy advantage
        
- - Death-themed characters
        
- - Long encounters

## [[Order of Scribes]]

[[Order of Scribes]] treats the Wizard's spellbook as a central part of their magical identity. The subclass emphasizes versatility, written magical knowledge, damage-type manipulation, and a spectral manifestation of the spellbook.

Its greatest strength is making the Wizard's already broad spellbook even more adaptable. It is less about committing to one damage type or combat role and more about exploiting the large number of spells available to a Wizard.

**Combat**

[[Order of Scribes]] benefits from having many different spells available. It can change how certain spells interact with damage types and use its spectral magical presence for additional utility.

The subclass is therefore strongest in the hands of a player who actively changes spell selection based on the encounter rather than using the same small group of spells every fight.

**Progression**

**Early:**  
Build a broad spellbook and experiment with different spell types.

**Mid:**  
Your spellbook becomes a major source of flexibility, letting you select appropriate tools for different encounters.

**End:**  
The subclass reaches its intended role as an extremely versatile Wizard capable of adapting its spellcasting to unusual situations.

**Best for:**

- - Spellbook-focused builds
        
- - Maximum versatility
        
- - Damage-type manipulation
        
- - Players who enjoy preparing different spells
        
- - Flexible arcane casters
        
- - Experimental builds

## [[Psionics]]

[[Psionics]] replaces conventional Wizard specialization with raw mental power. It combines arcane knowledge with telekinesis, psychic attacks, mental defenses, and direct manipulation of enemies and objects.

Rather than simply casting traditional elemental spells, this subclass provides an alternative method of controlling the battlefield through mental force.

**Combat**

[[Psionics]] can attack minds, move creatures or objects, establish defensive psionic effects, and control enemy positioning. This gives it a different approach to battlefield control from conventional Wizards.

Its strongest turns often involve combining normal Wizard spellcasting with psionic effects rather than treating psionics as a complete replacement for the spellbook.

**Progression**

**Early:**  
Use basic psychic and telekinetic effects alongside conventional Wizard spells.

**Mid:**  
Psionic control becomes more significant as enemies become tougher and positioning becomes more important.

**End:**  
The subclass becomes a powerful mental-control hybrid with enough tools to influence enemies without relying exclusively on traditional spell effects.

**Best for:**

- - Psychic damage

- - Telekinesis

- - Mental control

- - Psionic-themed characters

- - Battlefield manipulation

- - Alternative Wizard gameplay

## [[Transmutation]]

[[Transmutation]] specializes in changing the properties of creatures, objects, and the environment. It is less focused on direct damage and more interested in improving allies, altering physical conditions, crafting, and providing alchemical utility.

This makes it one of the strongest utility-oriented choices for players who want their Wizard to contribute outside combat as much as inside it.

**Combat**

[[Transmutation]] can improve allies, alter physical properties, manipulate forms, and use conventional Wizard spells for direct combat. Its value often comes from preparation rather than explosive turns.

The subclass can contribute to combat without needing every turn to be spent attacking, but it generally lacks the immediate offensive specialization of [[Evocation]].

**Progression**

**Early:**  
Use transmutation and utility effects to improve the party while relying on the normal Wizard spell list for combat.

**Mid:**  
Buffs, crafting, alchemy, and transformation effects become increasingly useful as the party gains access to more equipment and resources.

**End:**  
The subclass becomes a broad utility specialist capable of improving party efficiency both inside and outside combat.

**Best for:**

- Alchemical utility
- Buffing allies
- Transformation magic
- Crafting-focused characters
- Exploration utility
- Flexible support Wizards

## [[War Magic]]

[[War Magic]] is a battle-oriented Wizard built around surviving dangerous encounters while maintaining offensive spellcasting. It combines defensive reactions with aggressive magical tactics, making it more resilient than a conventional backline caster without becoming a dedicated tank.

The subclass is designed for Wizards who expect to be actively targeted and want defensive mechanics that directly support their ability to keep casting.

**Combat**

[[War Magic]] focuses on maintaining concentration, improving defenses, surviving incoming attacks, and responding to threats with reactions. It can still use the full Wizard spell list, so its offensive capabilities remain strong.

Its action economy is generally straightforward: cast a spell with the action, then use defensive or tactical abilities when necessary. This makes it easier to play than subclasses that require extensive setup or summon management.

**Progression**

**Early:**  
Focus on basic defensive tools and initiative while using conventional Wizard spells for damage and control.

**Mid:**  
Defensive mechanics become more important as enemy attacks become stronger and encounters become more dangerous.

**End:**  
The subclass becomes a reliable combat Wizard that can maintain strong offensive casting while being significantly harder to disrupt.

**Best for:**

- Combat-focused Wizards
- Defensive spellcasters
- Concentration-heavy builds
- Initiative-focused builds
- Aggressive casters
- Players who want a straightforward Wizard
