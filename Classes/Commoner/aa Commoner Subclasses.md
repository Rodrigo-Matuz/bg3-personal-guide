# [[Commoner]]

The [[Commoner]] is a deliberately unconventional class built around surviving adventure without the training or magical power of traditional adventurers. Instead of powerful spellcasting or specialized martial features, Commoners rely on **Grit**, practical skills, useful equipment, and their chosen profession. Grit can be spent to reduce incoming damage, improve attacks, and power certain subclass abilities. Commoners can also use Constitution for their AC through their natural toughness and use Wisdom with their signature [[Old Reliable]] weapon.

The class is weaker and more specialized than conventional adventuring classes, but it makes up for this with unusual utility and strong subclass identities. [[Farmer]] gains a scaling ox companion, [[Innkeeper]] provides social support and limited magical effects, [[Labourer]] becomes extremely durable and capable of carrying huge amounts of equipment, while [[Town Guard]] receives the most conventional martial training.

In combat, [[Commoner]] can fill several different roles depending on its subclass:

- **Support** — Helps allies through temporary HP, defensive effects, and limited magical abilities.
- **Tank** — Uses Grit, Constitution-based defenses, and damage resistance to survive attacks.
- **Martial combatant** — Uses weapons and limited combat maneuvers to fight directly.
- **Companion user** — Fights alongside a scaling animal companion.
- **Utility specialist** — Provides unusual exploration abilities and practical solutions.
- **Skill specialist** — Uses Tall Tales and subclass features to cover additional skills and non-combat situations.

## [[Farmer]]

The [[Farmer]] is the most companion-focused Commoner. Instead of becoming a conventional warrior, you rely on your **Loyal Livestock**, a hardy ox that accompanies you and becomes stronger as you progress. The subclass also provides practical support through produce and eventually gives you a way to recreate [[Plant Growth]] outside combat.

In exploration, the ox provides additional utility and companionship while your farming abilities give the class a strong practical identity. In combat, the ox is an additional body on the battlefield that can attack enemies while you support it and the rest of the party.

### Combat

The [[Farmer]] plays as a **support/companion hybrid**. Your ox gives you an additional combatant without requiring you to spend your own Action every turn to keep it attacking.

Your produce ability lets you spend an Action and Grit to give an ally temporary HP. This makes your Grit an important resource because it can either protect you through [[Gumption]] or provide additional durability to the party.

As the ox improves, it becomes a more meaningful contributor rather than simply a novelty. At the end of the progression, the Loyal Livestock gains additional HP and can attack twice per turn, giving the Farmer considerably more battlefield presence.

The main drawback is that much of your combat identity depends on the ox. If it is poorly positioned or unable to contribute, the Farmer loses a significant part of its value.

### Progression

**Early:** The Loyal Livestock gives you an immediate combat companion while Animal Handling and Wisdom-based utility improve exploration.

**Mid:** Produce-based temporary HP gives you a useful support option, allowing you to spend Grit to keep allies alive.

**End:** The ox becomes a stronger independent combatant with multiple attacks, while [[Plant Growth]] replication adds significant exploration utility outside combat.

**Best for:**

- Companion gameplay
- Support
- Animal-themed characters
- Temporary HP
- Unusual exploration utility

## [[Innkeeper]]

The [[Innkeeper]] is the social and support-oriented Commoner. Instead of relying on martial ability, it uses charm, insight, stories, and [[Grit]] to reproduce limited magical effects. This makes it the most naturally social Commoner and the subclass with the strongest non-combat utility.

In exploration, the Innkeeper is particularly useful for dialogue and social checks. Its minimum Insight roll makes it consistently reliable at reading situations, while its collection of spell-like abilities gives it practical tools without turning it into a conventional spellcaster.

### Combat

The [[Innkeeper]] is a **support and control specialist**. Its Grit-powered abilities can reproduce effects such as [[Friends]], [[Guidance]], [[Vicious Mockery]], [[Heroism]], [[Tasha's Hideous Laughter]], and [[Enthrall]].

This gives you several ways to contribute without relying on weapon damage. You can improve an ally's roll, interfere with an enemy, or provide defensive support depending on the situation.

Your main limitation is resource economy. These effects consume Grit, so you need to decide whether a particular magical effect is worth spending the same resource that can also help you survive damage.

At the end of the progression, the subclass becomes much better at supporting the entire party after rests. The party gains temporary HP, protection against [[Charmed]] and [[Frightened]], and additional saving throw support.

### Progression

**Early:** Strong Insight and Grit-powered utility make you an effective social character with several useful combat tricks.

**Mid:** More spell-like effects expand your ability to support allies and control enemies.

**End:** Reduced Grit costs and party-wide temporary HP make the Innkeeper a strong long-term support character, especially for parties that value defensive buffs.

**Best for:**

- Party face
- Social checks
- Support
- Crowd control
- Defensive buffs
- Players who want a non-traditional caster

## [[Labourer]]

The [[Labourer]] is the simplest and toughest Commoner subclass. It trades versatility for physical strength, durability, carrying capacity, and access to heavy weapons. This is the closest the Commoner gets to a straightforward physical bruiser.

In exploration, the subclass is excellent for carrying equipment and handling physical obstacles. Its fivefold increase to carrying capacity makes it particularly useful for characters who collect large quantities of gear.

### Combat

The [[Labourer]] is a **durable physical combatant**. It gains proficiency with mauls, warhammers, greataxes, and battleaxes, giving it access to much stronger weapons than the base Commoner.

Its resistance to Bludgeoning damage and an additional resistance based on its chosen type of labor significantly improves its durability.

The subclass does not gain the extensive offensive toolkit of a Fighter or Barbarian. Its strength comes from being difficult to kill and capable of using heavy weapons effectively.

This makes it straightforward to play: get into range, use your heavy weapon, and spend Grit when you need additional protection or combat reliability.

### Progression

**Early:** Heavy weapon proficiency and dramatically increased carrying capacity immediately define the subclass.

**Mid:** Damage resistances make you considerably tougher against common damage types.

**End:** Your physical durability remains the core strength, while [[Wall of Stone]]-like utility outside combat gives you a permanent environmental tool.

**Best for:**

- Heavy weapons
- Durability
- Carrying equipment
- Physical combat
- Simple frontline gameplay

## [[Town Guard]]

The [[Town Guard]] is the most traditionally martial Commoner. It represents someone with actual combat training without becoming a full [[Fighter]]. It gains medium armor, shields, a chosen martial weapon, and limited [[Battle Master]]-style maneuvers that use Grit instead of Superiority Dice.

In exploration, it has fewer unusual utility tools than the other Commoner subclasses, but it is the easiest Commoner to use as a conventional adventuring combatant.

### Combat

The [[Town Guard]] is a **martial hybrid** built around [[Old Reliable]]. Its weapon proficiency and armor access make it significantly better equipped for direct combat than the base Commoner.

Its Battle Master-style maneuvers give it additional ways to influence combat rather than simply making basic weapon attacks. These maneuvers use Grit, so your resource management directly affects both your offensive options and defensive survival.

[[Old Reliable]] is the subclass's defining weapon. Attacks with it gain an additional attack and damage bonus based on a Grit Die, making the weapon increasingly reliable as your character progresses. Later, the required roll for a critical hit is reduced by 1, further improving its offensive consistency.

The main limitation is that the Town Guard is still fundamentally a Commoner rather than a Fighter. It has useful martial tools, but it does not have the same depth of attacks, abilities, or specialization as a dedicated martial class.

### Progression

**Early:** Medium armor, shields, martial weapon proficiency, and Battle Master-style maneuvers give you a strong foundation for direct combat.

**Mid:** [[Old Reliable]] becomes more accurate and damaging through Grit, making your basic attacks more dependable.

**End:** Improved critical-hit range further increases the weapon's consistency, while your maneuvers and Grit provide additional tactical options.

**Best for:**

- Martial Commoners
- Shields
- Weapon combat
- Battle Master-style maneuvers
- Straightforward frontline gameplay

## Choosing an [[Commoner]]

|Subclass|Description|
|---|---|
|[[Farmer]]|Choose it if you want a loyal ox companion and a support-focused character with strong practical utility.|
|[[Innkeeper]]|Choose it if you want a social character that supports the party with Grit-powered magical effects.|
|[[Labourer]]|Choose it if you want a tough heavy-weapon user with massive carrying capacity and strong resistances.|
|[[Town Guard]]|Choose it if you want the most conventional martial Commoner with armor, shields, maneuvers, and an enhanced signature weapon.|

The [[Commoner]] is a deliberately unconventional class that turns ordinary skills, toughness, Grit, and practical professions into surprisingly useful adventuring tools.
