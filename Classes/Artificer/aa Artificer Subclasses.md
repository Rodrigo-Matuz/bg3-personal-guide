# [[Artificer]]

The [[Artificer]] is a versatile half-caster built around magical craftsmanship, equipment enhancement, and specialized inventions. Instead of relying entirely on spells or conventional weapons, the Artificer uses [[Infusion]] to improve equipment and gains subclass-specific tools that define how it contributes to combat. This gives the class strong party utility while still allowing it to specialize into damage, defense, support, or companion-based combat. The BG3 implementation includes four subclasses: [[Alchemist]], [[Armorer]], [[Artillerist]], and [[Battle Smith]].

[[Infusion]] is one of the class's main advantages. It allows the Artificer to enhance equipment rather than simply depending on whatever magical items happen to be found, making the class useful both for its own build and for improving other party members. The class also has access to offensive, defensive, and utility spells, giving it useful options when its specialized equipment is not the best solution.

The Artificer chooses its specialization at Level 3. From that point onward, the subclass provides the main combat identity of the character: alchemical support, magical armor, ranged artillery, or a mechanical companion.

In combat, [[Artificer]] can fill several different roles depending on its subclass:

- **Support** — Provides buffs, healing, consumables, and equipment enhancements.
- **Tank** — Uses magical armor, defensive abilities, and enemy-targeting effects to protect the party.
- **Ranged damage** — Uses magical weapons, spells, and an [[Eldritch Cannon]] to attack from safety.
- **Martial damage** — Combines weapons, Infusions, Extra Attack, and subclass abilities.
- **Companion controller** — Fights alongside a [[Steel Defender]] that adds another body and set of actions to the battlefield.
- **Utility specialist** — Uses crafted equipment, consumables, spells, and specialized tools to solve different problems.

## [[Alchemist]]

The [[Alchemist]] is the support-focused [[Artificer]], using experimental concoctions instead of relying exclusively on conventional spellcasting. Its strongest contribution comes from preparing and using specialized potions, elixirs, and other consumables that can improve the party's performance or solve specific problems.

In exploration, the Alchemist is particularly useful when you want a character who can prepare tools for an upcoming situation rather than simply reacting once combat begins. In combat, it generally works from the backline, using buffs, healing, utility effects, and its spellcasting while spending consumables when their specific effect is more valuable than a normal spell.

### Combat

The Alchemist is primarily a **support and utility character**. Its action economy is less focused on repeatedly dealing damage and more on choosing the correct consumable or spell for the current situation.

Experimental Alchemy gives the subclass additional ways to create useful concoctions, allowing it to stockpile effects that can be used when needed. This makes preparation important: the Alchemist is strongest when you understand what the next encounter requires and bring the appropriate tools.

Its spellcasting provides additional healing, buffs, and utility when consumables are not the best option. It can contribute damage when necessary, but it generally gets more value from enabling the rest of the party.

At higher progression, Chemical Mastery significantly improves its survivability and restorative capabilities, including Poison and Acid resistance and immunity to being Poisoned.

### Progression

**Early:** Establish yourself as a support character. Use Experimental Alchemy to produce useful consumables and supplement them with your normal spellcasting.

**Mid:** Your collection of alchemical options expands, allowing you to provide more specialized buffs, healing, and utility while still contributing with spells.

**End:** Chemical Mastery makes you substantially more resilient while improving your ability to restore and support the party. The subclass becomes a dedicated preparation and support specialist.

**Best for:**

- Party support
- Healing
- Buffs
- Consumable-focused gameplay
- Utility and preparation
- Players who enjoy having the right tool for the situation

## [[Armorer]]

The [[Armorer]] turns its armor into a magical piece of specialized equipment that determines how it fights. The subclass can configure its armor for either a durable frontline role or a more mobile ranged role, making it one of the most adaptable [[Artificer]] subclasses.

In exploration, the armor gives the character a distinct technological and magical identity while allowing the same character to change between defensive and offensive configurations. In combat, the choice between Guardian and Infiltrator determines whether you play as a frontline bruiser/tank or a mobile ranged attacker.

### Combat

The [[Armorer]] has two main combat configurations.

**Guardian Armor** is designed for frontline combat. Its Thunder Gauntlets allow you to attack enemies in melee while applying effects that discourage them from attacking your allies. Defensive abilities make the Guardian configuration substantially better at standing directly in front of dangerous enemies.

**Infiltrator Armor** changes the role completely. The Lightning Launcher gives you a ranged attack and makes the armor better suited to mobile combat and attacking from a safer distance.

This gives the Armorer more flexibility than most martial subclasses. You can build around protecting the party with Guardian Armor or switch toward ranged damage with Infiltrator Armor without abandoning the core Artificer toolkit.

### Progression

**Early:** Arcane Armor establishes your combat role. Guardian provides a durable melee option, while Infiltrator gives you a ranged alternative.

**Mid:** Extra Attack substantially improves the subclass's action economy. You can now make better use of your armor's weapon abilities while maintaining access to Artificer spells.

**End:** Your armor configurations become significantly stronger and more specialized. The subclass remains effective as either a frontline tank/bruiser or a ranged armored combatant.

**Best for:**

- Frontline tanking
- Defensive melee combat
- Ranged combat
- Flexible equipment-based builds
- Players who want magical power armor

## [[Artillerist]]

The [[Artillerist]] specializes in ranged magical firepower through its [[Eldritch Cannon]]. The cannon gives the subclass a second source of battlefield pressure while the Artificer itself remains free to cast spells or make ranged attacks.

In exploration, the subclass feels like a dedicated magical engineer whose primary invention is a deployable combat weapon. In combat, it operates best from the backline, positioning the cannon where it can repeatedly contribute while the Artificer uses its own actions for spells and attacks.

### Combat

The [[Eldritch Cannon]] is the center of the subclass's action economy. It gives you an additional combat tool that can attack enemies or provide defensive support depending on its configuration.

This is important because the cannon can consume your Bonus Action while your main Action remains available for spells, attacks, or other abilities. Instead of choosing between attacking and using your turret every turn, you can often combine both.

The cannon provides different functions, including offensive area damage and defensive support. This lets the Artillerist adjust its role depending on the encounter rather than simply using the same attack every round.

Your own spellcasting complements the cannon particularly well. Fire-based and area-damage spells give you strong options for attacking groups, while the cannon adds another layer of damage or protection.

Later progression allows multiple [[Eldritch Cannon]] summons, dramatically increasing the amount of battlefield presence the subclass can create.

### Progression

**Early:** The [[Eldritch Cannon]] immediately gives you a strong Bonus Action option and establishes the subclass as a ranged battlefield specialist.

**Mid:** Your cannon becomes more effective while your spellcasting provides increasingly strong ways to exploit its ranged and area-damage role.

**End:** Multiple cannons and stronger offensive tools allow you to control large portions of the battlefield while continuing to use your own Action for spells or attacks.

**Best for:**

- Ranged damage
- AoE damage
- Battlefield control
- Backline support
- Turret gameplay
- Players who enjoy managing multiple battlefield tools

## [[Battle Smith]]

The [[Battle Smith]] is the most directly martial [[Artificer]], combining magical equipment with a [[Steel Defender]]. The mechanical companion provides another body on the battlefield while the Artificer itself can fight with martial weapons, making the subclass effective in either melee or ranged combat.

In exploration, the Steel Defender gives the character a distinctive companion-based identity. In combat, the subclass revolves around coordinating your own attacks with the Defender's actions rather than treating the companion as a passive bonus.

### Combat

The [[Steel Defender]] is the centerpiece of the Battle Smith's action economy. It can attack enemies, occupy space, assist allies, and provide additional battlefield pressure while you use your own Action for weapon attacks or spells.

The subclass also receives Martial Weapon proficiency, making it the most naturally weapon-oriented Artificer. You can therefore build around conventional martial equipment while still benefiting from Infusions and Artificer spellcasting.

Extra Attack later gives the Battle Smith a significant improvement to its own sustained damage. At the same time, the Steel Defender continues providing an additional source of actions, making the subclass particularly effective when you can keep both characters active.

The main drawback is that the Defender needs to be managed. Positioning matters, and losing the companion removes a substantial part of the subclass's battlefield presence until it can be restored.

### Progression

**Early:** The Steel Defender gives you an immediate companion and additional battlefield presence, while Martial Weapon proficiency lets you fight effectively with conventional weapons.

**Mid:** Extra Attack improves your personal damage while the Steel Defender continues to contribute attacks and utility. The subclass becomes a true two-unit combat style.

**End:** Your martial capabilities and magical equipment are substantially stronger, while the Steel Defender remains an important source of damage, positioning, and battlefield utility.

**Best for:**

- Martial combat
- Companion gameplay
- Frontline fighting
- Ranged weapon builds
- Extra battlefield presence
- Players who want a mechanical pet

## Choosing an [[Artificer]]

| Subclass     | Description                                                                                           |   |
| ------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| [[Alchemist]]    | Choose it if you want a support specialist focused on healing, buffs, consumables, and utility.       |
| [[Armorer]]      | Choose it if you want magical armor with the flexibility to switch between tanking and ranged combat. |
| [[Artillerist]]  | Choose it if you want ranged magical damage, AoE, and battlefield control through deployable cannons. | 
| [[Battle Smith]] | Choose it if you want martial combat combined with a mechanical companion.                            |

The [[Artificer]] is a versatile magical engineer that combines spellcasting, enhanced equipment, and specialized inventions to adapt to almost any party role.
